## 1. First principle — what pedagogy is *for*

Pedagogy exists to cause **one thing: a durable, transferable change in what a
learner can do.** *Durable* = survives past the lesson; *transferable* = fires in a
new context, not only the taught one. This is the telos; everything else is
mechanism. (It is our own **coach-absent test** in the field's language: a change
visible only under the teacher's eye is performance, not learning.)

## 2. Five sub-problems any pedagogy must solve (first-principles decomposition)

Forced by the telos — exhaustive, not a menu:
1. **Intent** — *what* change? → **learning objectives** (you can't cause an
   unspecified change).
2. **Evidence** — how would we *know*? → **assessment** (an undetectable change
   can't be claimed).
3. **Activity** — what does the *learner* do to cause it? → **instruction /
   learning activity** (capability comes from the learner's activity, not the
   teacher's telling — **constructivism**).
4. **Sequence** — in what *order*, from the learner's current state? →
   **progression + scaffolding** (change builds on prior state).
5. **Coherence** — do 1–4 name the *same* thing? → **alignment** (the meta-constraint).

## 3. The ontology — where the field's terms live

| Term | Sub-problem | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| **Pedagogy** | the whole (1–5) + the *why* | "how do we cause learning, and why this way" — normative, learner-centered |
| **Curriculum** | intent + sequence (1 + 4) | **"*what* is learned, in *what order*"** — content & structure |
| **Didactics / instruction** | activity (3) | "*how* it's delivered" — methods (Continental tradition: a branch of pedagogy) |
| **Assessment** | evidence (2) | "how we *know* it worked" |
| **Constructive alignment** | coherence (5) | "do the above name the *same* skill?" |

**Curriculum is a *mechanism of* pedagogy** — the *what/sequence* mechanism inside
the encompassing intent-and-theory. A gorgeous curriculum can serve a broken
pedagogy; detecting that is the whole point of understanding the intent layer.

**Canon behind each sub-problem:**
- **Objectives (1):** Bloom's taxonomy (1956) — a hierarchy of objectives
  (cognitive · affective · psychomotor), lower→higher order; gives objectives a
  *verb vocabulary* (recall < apply < evaluate < create).
- **Assessment (2):** Scriven (1967) — **formative** (during, to correct) vs
  **summative** (terminal judgment); Bloom's **mastery learning** folds formative
  assessment + correction into the claim that *nearly all* learners can reach
  mastery.
- **Sequence (4):** Vygotsky's **Zone of Proximal Development** — the gap between
  unaided and aided performance; teach *at that edge*. **Scaffolding** = graduated
  help that **fades** as competence grows.
- **Coherence (5):** Biggs' **constructive alignment** + Wiggins & McTighe's
  **backward design** (*Understanding by Design*, 1998/2005) — start from the
  outcome, design the assessment, *then* the instruction.
- **Learner (adult):** Knowles' **andragogy** — adults are self-directed,
  experience-rich, problem-centered, internally motivated, need the *why*.
- **Skill acquisition:** Fitts-Posner (cognitive→associative→autonomous);
  Kirschner-Sweller-Clark (novices need *guidance*, not minimal-guidance discovery).

## 4. How they work hand-in-hand — the alignment triangle

Biggs' rule: **the verb in the objective must be *activated* in the activity and
*verified* in the assessment.**

```
        OBJECTIVE   ("the learner can falsify a claim")
          /                       \
   ACTIVITY  ———— same verb ———— ASSESSMENT
   (learner                       (measures the
    falsifies)                     learner falsifying)
```

Break the triangle — objective says *"falsify,"* activity is *"read about
falsification"* (exposition), assessment is *"MCQ about Popper"* (recall) — and the
delivery exercises and measures a *different skill than the intent*. **No polish
fixes misalignment.** Backward design is the discipline that prevents drift: fix the
objective → the assessment that proves it → the activity, in that order.

## 6. The evaluation lens — discerning whether a delivery serves the intent

Run these five on any proposed delivery (this is the tool the Operator asked for):
1. **Objective clear?** Target capability stated as a *verb* at the right Bloom level.
2. **Alignment triangle closed?** Activity *and* assessment exercise *that verb*.
   (The #1 failure mode.)
3. **Right level?** Pitched at the ZPD — not below (boredom) or above (overwhelm).
4. **Scaffolding that fades?** Support tapers toward independence (our "coach-absent").
5. **Assessment valid *and* honest?** Measures the objective; claims only what it
   earned (readiness vs mastery).
